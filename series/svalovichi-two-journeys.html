Svalovichi: Two Journeys

Some places call you back, not because they are scenic, but because they hold a quiet, fading truth. Svalovychi is a remote, nearly abandoned village in Ukraine, often called the “village of widows,” where only a handful of elderly residents remain.

This collection is a personal archive of two road trips taken in 2020 and 2021—same destination, but different routes, different companions, and a changing self. Ironically, the resulting album feels melancholic and detached, contrasting sharply with the lively, humor-filled atmosphere of the journeys themselves. Along the way, we met locals from dead-end villages—open, deeply hospitable people ready to give directions, track down a tractor for emergency repairs, or share whatever food they had.

The first trip took place during Easter week, surrounded by traditional Paska bread; the second was during the peak of COVID-19, which was invisible in the countryside except for curious questions about how the capital was coping. It is a lingering regret that my lens rarely touched these people; the camera was looking for something else.

Yet, these photographs, captured effortlessly without architectural pretense or artistic struggle, have become deeply personal. They document a slow, disappearing world under a vast sky, dust on the gravel roads, and the heavy silence of a place being reclaimed by time. It is a visual journal of a journey shared with friends, keeping alive a corner of the earth that is quietly vanishing.
